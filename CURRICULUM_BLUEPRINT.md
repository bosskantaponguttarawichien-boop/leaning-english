# Englist Communication-First Curriculum Blueprint

## 1. Learner and outcome

This curriculum is designed for Boss, an A1-A2 Thai learner who wants to use
English in daily life and at work, not prepare for a grammar examination.

The course must solve three recurring problems:

1. Grammar questions branch into too many details.
2. Knowledge is learned without a situation, so similar forms become confused.
3. Recognition is mistaken for ability: the learner can understand an answer
   but cannot retrieve it quickly in a real conversation.

The primary outcome is therefore:

> Communicate a clear message in a familiar real-life situation, then improve
> accuracy and naturalness through repeated retrieval.

## 2. Non-negotiable teaching sequence

Every regular lesson follows the same order:

1. **Mission** — one real situation and one observable can-do target.
2. **Vocabulary preview** — 8-12 existing words from `data/vocab.json`.
3. **Useful chunks** — 5-8 ready-to-use phrases, not isolated grammar rules.
4. **Tense lens** — one main time pattern and, only when needed, one contrast.
5. **Comprehensible input** — a short dialogue or story with a clear purpose.
6. **Notice** — identify the target pattern inside the input.
7. **Controlled practice** — classify, fill a gap, reorder, or transform.
8. **Shadowing** — listen and imitate meaning, rhythm, and pronunciation.
9. **Real output** — retell, roleplay, or respond with personal information.
10. **Exit check** — measure retrieval and task completion.

Vocabulary must appear before the story or dialogue. The vocabulary bank remains
the single source of truth and is not replaced by lesson-local duplicate entries.

## 3. Scope of tense teaching

The learner sees the traditional 12-tense map in Lesson 1, but initially learns
six of those forms plus the essential `be going to` construction:

1. Present Simple
2. Present Continuous
3. Past Simple
4. Past Continuous
5. `be going to`
6. `will`
7. Present Perfect

The course also teaches `can`, `could`, `should`, `have to`, and imperatives as
essential communication tools while making clear that they are not tenses.

Past Perfect is introduced only as recognition in the final integration stage.
Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Continuous, Future
Perfect, and Future Perfect Continuous remain map-level recognition topics until
there is a real need.

## 4. The question-parking rule

Grammar explanations are divided into three levels:

- **Use now:** changes the message or is needed for the lesson mission.
- **Notice later:** useful contrast, limited to one short note.
- **Reference only:** technical detail that is not required for production.

Each lesson must give a safe default sentence. For example:

- Default: `I'm excited.`
- Useful contrast: `I feel excited.` emphasizes the internal feeling.
- Reference only: a full linking-verb classification is not required to complete
  a travel conversation.

## 5. Course architecture

### Phase 1 — Build the map (A1)

| Lesson | Situation | Main language | Exit task |
|---|---|---|---|
| L01 | Understanding the course map | 12-tense overview; 7 priority forms | Choose the right time family for six messages |
| L02 | Introducing yourself | `be` + noun/adjective/place | Give a 45-second introduction |
| L03 | A normal weekday | Present Simple | Describe six daily actions |
| L04 | Asking about another person | Do/Does questions and negatives | Complete a six-turn getting-to-know-you exchange |
| L05 | What is happening now | Present Continuous | Describe the current scene |
| L06 | Usually vs right now | Present Simple vs Continuous | Contrast routine and current action |

### Phase 2 — Move through time (A1-A2)

| Lesson | Situation | Main language | Exit task |
|---|---|---|---|
| L07 | Talking about yesterday | Past Simple regular verbs | Give a short yesterday update |
| L08 | Talking about last weekend | Common irregular past verbs | Tell a 60-second weekend story |
| L09 | An interruption or problem | Past Continuous + Past Simple | Explain what was happening when a problem occurred |
| L10 | Personal plans | `be going to` | State three intended plans and reasons |
| L11 | Appointments and arrangements | Present Continuous for future | Arrange a meeting or meal |
| L12 | Decisions, offers, predictions | `will` | Respond naturally to four changing situations |
| L13 | Life experience | Present Perfect: experience | Ask and answer `Have you ever...?` |
| L14 | A recent result | Present Perfect vs Past Simple | Explain a current problem and when it happened |
| L15 | Time and duration | `for`, `since`, `ago`, `about` | Explain how long three real activities have lasted |

### Phase 3 — Handle real conversations (A2)

| Lesson | Situation | Main language | Exit task |
|---|---|---|---|
| L16 | Ability, need, and advice | can/could/should/have to | Ask for help and give a practical recommendation |
| L17 | Clarifying when listening fails | repair phrases | Recover a conversation without switching to Thai |
| L18 | Feelings and opinions | be/feel + adjective; -ed/-ing adjectives | Explain a feeling and its cause |
| L19 | Airport and check-in | integrated present/future; polite requests | Complete an airport check-in roleplay |
| L20 | Taxi and directions | requests, place language, past retell | Reach a destination and retell a misunderstanding |
| L21 | Restaurant and shopping problem | requests, countability, problem language | Order, clarify, and solve one mistake politely |

### Phase 4 — Use English at work (A2-B1)

| Lesson | Situation | Main language | Exit task |
|---|---|---|---|
| L22 | Small talk and work status | Answer + Detail + Question | Hold a one-minute colleague conversation |
| L23 | Explaining a bug | past/current/result language | Give a five-part bug report |
| L24 | Integrated real-life challenge | seven priority forms | Complete a three-minute personal/work scenario |

## 6. Lesson content standard

Each lesson contains:

- 8-12 referenced vocabulary items from the preserved vocabulary database
- 5-8 useful chunks
- one short, practical grammar explanation
- one original dialogue or story of 70-150 words
- at least two recognition or controlled-practice activities
- a shadowing set of 4-8 sentences
- one personal or situational output activity
- Thai support for instructions and difficult meaning
- a model answer that can be used after the learner attempts the task

Content should use mostly known language:

- 90% familiar language in input
- no more than 10-12 pre-taught items per lesson
- one main tense target per lesson
- no more than one tense contrast per lesson

## 7. Weekly study plan

Recommended pace: 30 minutes per day, five days per week, two lessons per week.

### Day A — Understand

- Preview vocabulary
- Read the mission
- Study the tense lens
- Read/listen to the dialogue

### Day B — Notice and control

- Retrieve vocabulary without Thai
- Identify the tense in context
- Complete controlled exercises

### Day C — Listen and shadow

- Listen with text
- Repeat sentence by sentence
- Shadow at natural speed
- Record or self-check one attempt

### Day D — Use

- Retell or roleplay without reading
- Personalize at least three sentences
- Compare with the model only after attempting

### Day E — Retrieve and measure

- Repeat the exit task without notes
- Review weak chunks
- Revisit due vocabulary through SRS

## 8. Mastery and measurement

A lesson is ready for review when the learner can:

- recognize at least 80% of the target vocabulary
- complete controlled practice at 80% or higher
- use at least four target chunks without copying
- begin a response within five seconds
- complete the situation even with minor grammar errors
- rate speaking confidence at 3/5 or higher

Mastery is not “read and click next.” A lesson enters review after the first
completion and becomes mastered after a later successful retrieval check.

Monthly measurement uses four observable dimensions:

| Dimension | Measure |
|---|---|
| Comprehensibility | Can a listener understand the intended message? |
| Retrieval | Can the learner begin within five seconds? |
| Range | How many target chunks are used independently? |
| Accuracy | Does the target tense preserve the intended time meaning? |

## 9. Product changes required

1. Preserve `data/vocab.json` without content deletion or replacement.
2. Add a lesson vocabulary activity that reads word details from the existing
   vocabulary database.
3. Add working Shadowing and Roleplay renderers; these types currently exist in
   the schema but are not rendered by the lesson player.
4. Rewrite `data/curriculum.json` using the 24-lesson architecture above.
5. Update the lesson catalog so the situation, tense focus, and can-do outcome
   are visible before starting.
6. Change completion behavior so a first pass becomes `review`, and reserve
   `mastered` for a successful delayed retrieval.
7. Validate every vocabulary reference and every activity at build time.

## 10. Content QA checklist

Before release:

- Every referenced vocabulary item exists in `data/vocab.json`.
- Every lesson begins with vocabulary before input.
- Every lesson has a real situation and observable exit task.
- All English examples are grammatical and natural for the stated situation.
- Tense labels match the actual forms used.
- Thai explanations describe use, not only translation.
- All answer keys match their activities.
- All activity types render without a skip/unsupported fallback.
- The original vocabulary database count and checksum are unchanged.
- TypeScript, lint, curriculum validation, vocabulary validation, and production
  build all pass.
